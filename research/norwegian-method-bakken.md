# Norwegian Method (Marius Bakken): rules and workouts

- Date: 2026-11-23
- Question: What is the Norwegian Method as Marius Bakken describes and applies it? Turn it into a
  rule set and a workout library the app can use, and compare it with `lib/plan.ts`.
- Workout library as data: [`norwegian-method-bakken.workouts.json`](norwegian-method-bakken.workouts.json)

## Answer

Two **double threshold** days a week (Tue and Thu in the standard week), each a morning session of
long reps and an evening session of short reps. Both are run as intervals, never continuous, in a narrow
**2–3 mmol/l** lactate band ("Golden Zone" 2.3–3.0). Add one **X session** (short hills or fast
200s, above threshold but controlled) on Saturday. Every other run is truly easy (**<1.0 mmol/l,
<70% HRmax**), there's always an easy day after a threshold day, the long run is short (≤16–18 km,
about an hour), and volume is ~180 km/week for elites. In the race season, doubles become mostly singles and
race-pace checks are added. [1][2][3]

## 1. Rules

### Intensity

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| I1 | Threshold work sits at **2–3 mmol/l**, most of it 2.3–3.0, not the textbook 4.0. Lower lactate allows far more threshold volume without wearing down. | [1][3] |
| I2 | Without a lactate meter: **~80–87% HRmax** (well-trained), and the **talk test** (short sentences, no comfortable chat). Cross-check HR, talk test and feel. When they disagree, slow down. The less accurate the method, the bigger the safety margin. | [3] |
| I3 | **Morning (long reps): float just below threshold.** If lactate drifts above 3 mmol/l by the last rep, it was too hard. | [1][3] |
| I4 | **Evening (short reps): at or very slightly above threshold** (~3.0). Feeling good in the evening is *not* a reason to go harder. | [1][2][3] |
| I5 | Morning and evening paces differ by **~15–20 s/km**, both still in the threshold zone. | [3] |
| I6 | Three ways to run a session, all used at different times: *progressive* (2.0 → 3.0 over the session), *at the line* (hold just under 3.0), *floating* (0.4–0.8 below threshold; use after altitude or when tired). | [1] |
| I7 | **Easy means easy:** <1.0 mmol/l, <70% HRmax. Avoid the zone between easy and threshold for 5k/10k training (the marathon is the exception). | [1] |
| I8 | **X session:** above threshold but controlled, either short hills or 200–1000 m reps at **5–8 mmol/l**. | [1][2] |
| I9 | **Green, yellow and red light:** low warm-up HR and low lactate at speed means extend the session (green). A high warm-up HR, or HR that won't rise at the target lactate by rep 2–3, means stop early (red). | [1] |

### Session structure

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Intervals, not continuous**, for 1500 m–10k. The short recoveries lower muscle tone, keep lactate in range and make doubles repeatable. (The marathon needs more continuous work.) | [1][2][3] |
| S2 | **Rep length 30 s to 10 min.** Recoveries for reps ≤1 min: **15–30 s**. | [1] |
| S3 | **Morning = longer reps (6–10 min, 2000–3000 m). Evening = shorter reps (400–1000 m, 1 min, 45/15).** The reverse order is possible but morning-long is recommended. | [1][3] |
| S4 | **6–8 h between morning and evening.** Under 6 h loses some of the benefit. Over 8–10 h they become two separate workouts. | [3] |
| S5 | Treat the double day as *one long session with a rest break*. Keep each half moderate rather than one huge single session. | [2][3] |
| S6 | Little warm-up or cool-down is needed for threshold sessions. | [1] |

### Week structure

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| W1 | Standard base week: **Mon easy×2 · Tue double T · Wed easy×2 · Thu double T · Fri easy×2 · Sat X (+ easy) · Sun long run.** | [2] |
| W2 | **Always an easy day after a threshold or double-threshold day.** At least one full easy day between double days isn't optional. | [1][3] |
| W3 | **No threshold every day.** Little extra effect, much greater overtraining risk. | [1][3] |
| W4 | Fit the arrangement to your life: e.g. both doubles on the weekend, or Tue + Sat. Four threshold sessions a week is the constant, not the weekdays. | [3] |
| W5 | **Long run ≤16–18 km** (Bakken, 180 km/week), "up to one hour is enough" for middle distance. | [1][2] |
| W6 | **Weekly volume:** ~180 km for elites (5k range 150–220). 120 km is the minimum for many long and middle-distance runners, and 160–180 km works best for most elites. | [1] |
| W7 | Threshold share ≈ **23–25% of the week**. The 2004 week has about 40–45 km of reps in 180 km. *(Calculated from [2].)* | [2] |
| W8 | Strength and plyometrics: do them year-round rather than starting mid-season, ideally on threshold days (start or end), and plyometrics after the X session. | [1] |

### Periodization and race season

| # | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Flat weeks:** Bakken dropped "2 hard + 1 easy" for structurally similar weeks, with variation over the season and from altitude. If not using altitude, an alternative is 10–14 days of maximum threshold load, then 7 easier days. | [1][2] |
| P2 | **Vary within the threshold zone** (rep length, recovery, lactate 1.8–3.2 around a 3.0 threshold, terrain, treadmill) because the model has only two main speeds. | [1] |
| P3 | **Build vs sustain:** in a build, push longer reps and both sessions towards the top of the band. In season, shorten sessions, refuel between them, and widen the gap between AM and PM intensity. | [3] |
| P4 | **Race season:** cut threshold volume, with mostly single threshold sessions and occasional doubles. Add track work gradually: start with 300–400 m at 6–8 mmol, lengthen towards 1000 m, then 200–1000 m at 7–10. Back off if lactate climbs steeply at the same pace. | [1][2] |
| P5 | **Race-week checks:** 4 days before, 4–6 × 1000 m + 3–7 × 300 m at about 3k pace (watch the lactate curve). 2 days before, a standard threshold run. Optionally 2–3 × 2000 m threshold as part of the race warm-up. | [2] |
| P6 | After races, short easy-ish threshold work near 2.0 mmol (same day or next morning) keeps the volume up. | [2] |
| P7 | Before an important session or race, the last quality session was often 200 m reps. Very hard work (>10–12 mmol) should be rare and needs long recovery. | [1][2] |

## 2. Workout library

Paces: Bakken gives lactate and HR, not race paces. The race-pace column is an **inference**
for runners without a meter. It uses the app's existing bands (`lib/vdot.ts:77-81`) and I5's
15–20 s/km gap. Treat it as a starting point and adjust by I2.

### Morning threshold (long reps)

| ID | Session | Recovery | Target | ≈ Pace (inference) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AM1 | 6 × 2000 m | ~60 s jog | 2–3 mmol | HM–25k pace | [2][3] |
| AM2 | 4 × 10 min | ~60 s | 2–3 mmol | 25k–30k pace | [2] |
| AM3 | 5–6 × 6 min | ~60 s | 2.5–3 mmol | HM pace | [1] |
| AM4 | 3–4 × 3000 m | 60–90 s | 2–3 mmol | 25k–30k pace | [3] |
| AM5 | 3 × 10 min (split double, see the NTNU protocol) | 2 min | 2–3 mmol | 25k–30k pace | [3] |

### Evening threshold (short reps)

| ID | Session | Recovery | Target | ≈ Pace (inference) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PM1 | 20–25 × 400 m | 30 s jog | ~3.0 mmol | 15k–HM pace | [2][3] |
| PM2 | 10–12 × 1000 m | 60 s | ~3.0 mmol | 15k–HM pace | [2] |
| PM3 | 10–15 × 1 min | 30 s | ~3.0 (end slightly over) | 15k pace | [1] |
| PM4 | 15–20 × 45 s / 15 s | continuous | ~3.0 (end slightly over) | 15k pace | [1] |

### X session (one a week, Saturday)

| ID | Session | Recovery | Target | ≈ Pace (inference) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| X1 | 10–20 × 180–200 m short steep hill | jog down | strong, relaxed; 5–8 mmol | effort only | [1][2] |
| X2 | 6–8 × 600–800 m long hill | jog down | 5–8 mmol | effort only | [2] |
| X3 | 10 × 200 m (Bakken: 26–28 s) | ~200 m jog | fast, relaxed | mile–1500 m pace | [2] |
| X4 | 6–10 × 300–400 m / 5–8 × 1000 m flat | 1–2 min | 5–8 mmol | 3k–5k pace | [1] |

### Race season

| ID | Session | Recovery | Target | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | 4–6 × 1000 m + 3–7 × 300 m, **4 days before a race** | ~1–2 min | ~3k pace, watch the curve | [2] |
| R2 | Standard threshold run, **2 days before a race** | — | 2–3 mmol, check HR vs pace | [2] |
| R3 | 300–400 m reps, early season | — | 6–8 mmol | [2] |
| R4 | 200–1000 m mixed, later season | — | 7–10 mmol | [2] |
| R5 | 2–3 × 2000 m threshold in the race warm-up | — | 2–3 mmol | [2] |
| R6 | Short threshold the morning after a race | — | ~2.0 mmol | [2] |

### Sample weeks

**Base (Bakken, Feb 2004, 180 km):** [2]
Mon easy + easy · Tue AM1 / PM1 · Wed easy + easy · Thu AM2 / PM2 · Fri easy + easy · Sat X3 (+ easy) · Sun long run ≤16–18 km.

**Kristensen 2006 (178 km):** [1] Mon 15 + 12 easy · Tue T + T (+ strength) · Wed 16 + 10 easy ·
Thu T + T · Fri 15 + 9 easy (+ strength) · Sat hill X + 10 easy · Sun 21 easy.

**Race week (race Sat), inferred from P4–P5:** Mon easy · Tue R1 · Wed easy · Thu R2 · Fri easy + strides · Sat race · Sun easy or R6.

## Impact on the app

Code: `lib/plan.ts:300-366` (`bakkenSessions`), `lib/settings.ts:92` (default template).

**Matches:**
- Default week `easy, double, easy, double, easy, x, longrun` (`lib/settings.ts:92`) = W1.
- AM slower than PM: AM HM–30k pace, PM 15k–HM, never faster than threshold (`lib/plan.ts:318-319`). Fits I3–I5.
- Threshold share 25% (`lib/settings.ts:84`) ≈ W7. 15–40 min of reps per session (`plan.ts:318`) fits the 2004 sessions.
- AM 6/8/10 min reps, 60 s jog. PM 1 km/60 s and 400 m/30 s (`plan.ts:302-308`). Hills X with jog-down (`plan.ts:343-348`).
- Fewer doubles below a mileage threshold or in deload and taper weeks (`plan.ts:171`), in the spirit of P4.

**Differs:**
1. **PM 2 km reps** (`plan.ts:306`, `{ km: 2, …}`): evening sessions should be *short* (400–1000 m) per S3. Suggest replacing it with 1 min/30 s or 45/15 (PM3/PM4).
2. **45/15 is used as an X session** (`plan.ts:350-354`) at hills pace. Bakken's 45/15 is *evening threshold* work (~3 mmol), not the X element. Suggest moving it into `PM_FORMATS` and using X3 (10 × 200 m fast) instead.
3. **X flat 300s/400s at 10k–15k pace** (`plan.ts:356-359`, `hills` band = 10k–15k in `vdot.ts:81`). 5–8 mmol is closer to 3k–5k pace. Suggest a faster band for flat X reps.
4. **AM 5 min reps** (`plan.ts:302`): Bakken's morning range is 6–10 min. Minor. Suggest swapping in 2000 m or 3000 m reps.
5. **Long run = 22% of the week, up to 32 km** (`plan.ts:256`). Bakken caps it at 16–18 km / ~1 h (W5). At 180 km the app gives 32 km. Suggest a Bakken-specific cap of ~18 km, or ~60–75 min.
6. **Deload every 4th week** (`plan.ts:148`, `DELOAD_FACTOR`). Bakken prefers flat weeks (P1). Suggest making deloads off by default for the Norwegian Method, or offering 10–14 days on / 7 easier.
7. **No race-week checks:** the app doesn't schedule R1 (1000s + 300s, 4 days out) or R2 (threshold, 2 days out). The race-week code (`plan.ts:176-199`) clears quality within 2 days of a race. That conflicts with R2, which Bakken did "before all major races".
8. **No easy-day HR cap shown:** consider showing "<70% HRmax" on easy days and "80–87% HRmax" on threshold sessions in the workout text (I2, I7).
9. **Volume:** the app ramps up to 5% a week to any target. Bakken gives no ramp rule (see open questions).

## Open questions

- **Lower-mileage and amateur scaling.** Bakken's new book *The Norwegian Method Applied* covers "every level", but it wasn't available here. The web articles describe ~180 km elites. A Science of Running summary (May 2026) wasn't readable. Scaling (e.g. fewer doubles below ~60–80 km) stays an app-side choice until checked against the book.
- **Recoveries for AM reps** aren't stated in the articles. 60 s is the common practice and matches the app.
- **Hill gradient** for X1 isn't given ("short, steeper sprints" of 180–200 m).
- **Sub-threshold vs "Norwegian Singles":** the app's other method (sirpoc84) is an amateur adaptation, not Bakken's. Don't mix their rules.
